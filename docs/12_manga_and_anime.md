# 12 · Manga and Anime — two sources, two grammars

**Guide v3.2 · law revision v5.6 · added 2026-10-25.** Doc [`11`](11_register_and_camera.md) fixed *what
a sentence may contain*. This one fixes **which medium you are writing from**, because they are not the
same and the difference is not decoration.

---

## §1 — The correction, in the author's words

A chapter had been rewritten twice: once for **register** (dialogue, refrains, spell calls), once for
**style** (the camera law, N1–N5). Both were green. The author came back a third time:

> **"you take manuha okay but i also show, i mean by it anime style is, you need to understand"**

**Read correctly: he had asked for the anime, and I had been writing a manga and calling it canon.**

And he was right, because I had built caption boxes that label places — `> HAGE. THE FORSAKEN REALM.` /
`> MARCH. THE GRIMOIRE TOWER.` — which is a **manga device used as a chapter label.** The anime does not
do that. The anime finds a scene with **a sound**.

---

## §2 — The one sentence

> ### A manga is a series of chosen instants. An anime is a series of imposed durations.

**A manga reader controls time.** A panel is frozen; the gutter between two panels is where the reader
does the work — for a second or for a minute. The artist chooses *which* moments get to exist.

**An anime director controls time.** You cannot linger on a frame. You cannot skip the silence. **So the
anime has to fill duration** — and every device it has that the manga does not is a way of filling time.

| The manga has | The anime has, and the manga cannot |
|---|---|
| a panel | **a shot that lasts** — and holding a shot is a decision |
| a gutter | **a cut** — which you do not control |
| an SFX drawn on the page | **actual sound**, which carries the cut |
| a typeset bubble | **a performance** — breath, cracks, a voice running out of air |
| a caption box | **a voiceover over moving images** |
| — | **music — and the moment the music stops** |

**The failure mode this produces:** a chapter written from a manga reads like *a list of things that are
visible.* A chapter written from an anime reads like **something you are watching.**

---

## §3 — The X-series: six laws of the moving source

### X1 — Sound leads the cut
**Every scene arrives as a sound before it arrives as a picture.** An anime cannot cut silently, so the
prose gives you what you hear first.

- ✗ `> MARCH. THE GRIMOIRE TOWER.` then people walking.
- ✓ *Somebody's cart has a bad spot in one wheel and it goes round and round.*

- **Check:** a scene heading that labels a place, with no sound in the first paragraph, is a **manga
  caption** and should be cut.
- **Paid for by:** *Black Clover*, 2026-10-25 — five caption boxes in one chapter.

### X2 — The hold
**The anime can hold a face and do nothing with it. A manga panel cannot — an empty panel is a wasted
panel.** Silence, therefore, is material only animation owns.

- ✗ *"I'm sorry, Asta. But I'm everyone's sister."* — answered immediately.
- ✓ *She does not turn round.* / *"…"* / *The bucket comes up out of the well and stops at the top.* / *"I'm sorry, Asta…"*

**That is a two-second held shot, and it is the only reason the line lands.**

- **Check:** count the still paragraphs — no dialogue, no movement, no new information. **A scene with
  zero holds was written from a manga.**
- **Paid for by:** *Black Clover* (zero holds in the manga draft; six in the episode).

### X3 — The narrowing, not the cut
A manga goes **wide panel → close-up panel**: two panels, one gutter. **The anime does not cut there, it
moves** — one continuous push from the wide to the hand to the knuckles.

- ✗ *She lifted her hand. Water came up over her palm.*
- ✓ *Her hand comes up. Water stands over her palm. **It turns, and turns again, and goes white.***

- **Check:** does the scene reach its detail through **movement** or through a **jump**? Movement is the
  anime; a jump is a panel break.
- **Paid for by:** *Black Clover*.

### X4 — Breath in the line
**Manga dialogue is typeset. Anime dialogue is performed.** A line that is *said* has places where the
voice runs out.

- `…` is **a held shot**, not punctuation.
- A scream can crack mid-word: `"SIS—TER—LI—LY!"`
- A voice can be winded and keep going: *"GAH! … NGH—"*
- `"Ah—"` and `"Um—"` are **interruption**, and interruption is a timing no panel can hold.

- **Check:** is any line broken where a **body** breaks it? If every line is a complete sentence, it was
  transcribed, not performed.
- **Paid for by:** *Black Clover*.

### X5 — Episode architecture
An episode is not a chapter. It has a shape, and the shape is part of the style:

```
COLD OPEN   — the voiceover over an image, before you know whose story this is
A-part      — the story starts
EYECATCH    — a card, a beat, the middle
B-part      — the story continues
NEXT TIME   — a narrator tells you what is coming
```

**The cold open is why canon's legend lands.** In the manga it is a caption on page one. In the anime it
is **a voice over a battlefield, before the title.**

- **Check:** the three markers are **all present or all absent** — never two of three, which is a chapter
  that started as a manga and put a soundtrack on halfway.
- **Paid for by:** *Black Clover*.

### X6 — The moving source expands; it does not compress
**Anime episode one adapts manga chapter one and goes past it** — through the antagonist, to the artefact,
to the end-of-episode image. It does not cut canon to fit; **it makes room.**

**Therefore an anime-styled chapter is usually LONGER than a manga-styled one.** If your episode chapter
is shorter than your chapter chapter, you compressed — and episode writers do not compress.

- **Check:** the canon beats consumed against the episode's own running order. **A claim of `EP. N` that
  stops where the chapter stopped is a false card.**
- **Paid for by:** *Black Clover* — the card said `ep. 1` and the chapter stopped at the four-leaf, while
  ep. 1 runs to the five-leaf and the sword. **Declared in the footer, not silently fixed.**

## §4 — The proof: five pairs, same beat, two machines

**Nothing happens differently in any pair. Only the medium changes.**

### Pair 1 · The opening

**MANGA** — a caption, then a panel.

> `> MARCH. THE GRIMOIRE TOWER. THE GRIMOIRE ACCEPTANCE CEREMONY.`
> They walked up in the morning with the seed-fluff in the road.

**ANIME** — a voice, then a sound, then an image.

> `## COLD OPEN`
> *[voiceover]* LONG AGO, HUMANITY CAME CLOSE TO BEING WIPED OUT BY THE DEMONS.
> Wind across a field. / Then nothing. / A hill. A road going up it. Nobody on the road. / **Then, from the
> other side of the hill, a voice.** / "SIS—TER—LI—LY!" / The birds come up off the grass all at once and go wide.

**What changed:** the caption became a **voice**, and the scene arrived as **sound**. The voice is heard
before the boy is seen — a thing only the anime can do.

### Pair 2 · The refusal

**MANGA** — she answers.

**ANIME** — *she does not turn round. "…" The bucket comes up and stops at the top. Then she answers.*

**What changed:** **the hold.** Two panels cannot hold; a shot can.

### Pair 3 · The technique

**MANGA** — *She lifted her hand. Water came up out of nothing over her palm.* → spell call.

**ANIME** — *Her hand comes up. Water stands over her palm. **It turns, and turns again, and goes white.***
→ two more lines of dialogue → *then* the spell call → *and after the impact:* **the sound of it goes down
the street and comes back off the houses.**

**What changed:** the **narrowing** replaced the cut, the spell was **held back** so it lands on the shout,
and the **sound after the hit** did what a drawn SFX cannot.

### Pair 4 · Silence

**MANGA** — *The yard went quiet.*

**ANIME** — *The yard goes quiet. The little ones stop running. **A bucket stops swinging.*** — and later,
at the tower: **"Every sound in the hall stops. Not quiet. Stopped."**

**What changed:** the anime **mixes** sound, so silence is something you can hear stop. **"Not quiet.
Stopped."** is a sound edit in two words, and it renders the supernatural event as **audio** — which is
exactly how the anime would do it.

### Pair 5 · The cut home

**MANGA** — a rule, then *Night. The church is quiet.*

**ANIME** — *Sunset. The yard is orange and the post is a black line in it. … The post hits the dirt.
**Once. Twice.** **The light goes off the yard.*** → `## EYECATCH` → *The four-leaf clover on a black
field.*

**What changed:** the scene does not end, it **fades** — on a sound and a change of light — and there is
an eyecatch, because that is where an episode puts one.

## §5 — The numbers

| | Manga-styled | **Anime-styled** |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence average | 8.92 | **8.51** |
| Longest sentence | 34 | **34** |
| Dialogue share | 60% | **55%** |
| Caption boxes | 5 | **1 that sets a scene** |
| Sound beats before a cut | 0 | **every scene** |
| Held paragraphs | 0 | **6** |
| Push-ins / narrowings | 0 | **4** |
| Episode markers | none | **cold open · eyecatch · next time** |

**Read the caption row.** The manga version *labels its places*. The anime version has **one caption — the
legend — and finds every other scene with a sound.** That is the difference between **a book of places**
and **a thing that is playing.**

---

## §6 — Neither is better. The card decides.

| Write the **manga** register when | Write the **anime** register when |
|---|---|
| the canon base is a **chapter** | the canon base is an **episode** |
| the chapter is one place, one conversation | the chapter moves, and time passes inside it |
| you want the reader to **hold** a moment | you want the reader **pulled forward** |
| the beat **is** the point | the **sequence** is the point |

**And the house rule that settles it:** the chapter card says what you drew on. **`ANIME BASE: ep. N`
obliges the anime's grammar.** A chapter that claims an episode must be an episode — **not a chapter with
a soundtrack written into it.**

---

## §7 — Why this belongs in the guide

Every other chapter here is about a law that was paid for by a failure. **This is the cheapest one to
avoid and the most expensive one to miss**, because the failure is silent:

- the prose reads well,
- every gate is green,
- **and the medium is wrong.**

**You cannot gate your way out of writing the wrong object.** `camera_gate.py` checks N1–N5 and cannot
know whether you were looking at a page or a screen. **The card is the only instrument that catches it** —
which is why F4 and the canon-base line exist, and why this chapter is four sections of craft followed by
one question: **what did you actually watch, or what did you actually read?**
